# The Glade — Brand Brief

A reference document for setting up the Claude Design system. Captures *what* The Glade feels like before *how* it looks. Pair with `design-system.md` (the technical spec) and `wordmark.md`.

---

## One-line identity

**The Glade is a dark fantasy world at insect scale, where Miyazaki whimsy and Del Toro horror share the same forest floor.**

---

## The tonal axis

Every design choice should sit deliberately on a single axis:

```
woodland whimsy ◄──────────────────────────► gothic horror
   (Miyazaki, Hollow Knight overworld)        (Del Toro, Hollow Knight depths)
```

- **Default position:** dead center, leaning slightly gothic. Grounded but not dreary.
- **Lean whimsical** for: friendly NPC cards, market scenes, spring-zone material, player-facing handouts where joy is the point.
- **Lean gothic** for: combat encounters, corruption/cordyceps content, fall-zone material.
- **Never:** cartoon-cute. Never: grimdark-edgelord. The Glade is sad and warm at the same time.

The horror in The Glade is **quiet**. It announces itself in absences — a scentless forest, a wrenched-off door, a farm frozen mid-harvest.

---

## Audience and use

These designs are produced for **two co-DMs running the campaign**, viewed primarily on **screen at the table** (laptop and tablet).

**Primary outputs to optimize for:**
1. **Stat blocks & encounter sheets** — DM-facing, information-dense, scanned mid-combat. Legibility is non-negotiable.
2. **NPC / creature cards** — single-page reference: portrait, disposition, hooks, voice notes.

**Secondary outputs (later):** session recap headers, player-facing handouts, maps.

**Hard requirements that flow from "DM at the table on a tablet":**
- Dark backgrounds preferred (low ambient light, glare-friendly).
- Numbers and dice notation must read at a glance — use a tabular/mono face for stat values.
- Color is meaningful, not decorative. Each accent encodes a thing (see palette in `design-system.md`).
- Never sacrifice scannability for atmosphere. Atmosphere lives in the frame, the texture, the title — not the stat values.

---

## Voice

The voice on the page should match the voice at the table.

- **Plain words for hard things.** "The doors were wrenched off. Hinges bent outward." not "There was evidence of forced ingress."
- **Sensory specifics over adjectives.** Smells, sounds, temperature, what the antennae pick up before the eyes do.
- **Bug-scale framing.** A raindrop is a falling rock. A puddle is a sticky trap. A scorpion's stinger is a museum piece. Lean into this constantly — it's the campaign's defining lens.
- **Wry, not flippant.** Humor lands as undercurrent, not punchline.
- **No high-fantasy bombast.** No "verily," no "thee," no proclamations. Bugs are tired and trying.

## Mood references — what to extract from each

Claude Design works best with real examples and clear instruction on *what* to take from each. Treat these as anchors, not templates.

### Hollow Knight (primary visual anchor)
**Take:** silhouette-forward bug character design, painterly muted backgrounds, hand-drawn UI ornament, restraint with detail.
**Don't take:** the specific glowing-blue palette wholesale, or the platformer-game UI layout.

### Princess Mononoke / Nausicaä (Miyazaki)
**Take:** sense of scale (small things in vast forests), the dignity of small creatures, lush but slightly threatening greenery, weather as character, the kodama as a model for "uncanny but not malevolent."
**Don't take:** anime stylization, bright saturation.

### Pan's Labyrinth / Crimson Peak (Del Toro)
**Take:** insects-as-craftwork (the elaborate beetles and fairy creatures), warm browns and sickly golds, organic ornament that's beautiful *and* slightly wrong, candlelit interiors.
**Don't take:** human gore, period-piece literalism.

### A Bug's Life / Microcosmos (the bug-scale lens)
**Take:** the conviction that an acorn cap is a roof and a leaf is a sail — physical-scale plausibility. The textural fidelity of real bug bodies in *Microcosmos* is gold.
**Don't take:** the Pixar-cute character design from *A Bug's Life*. Our bugs are real bugs, not anthropomorphized ones.

### Mörk Borg, Mothership, Trophy Gold (RPG zine layouts)
**Take:** confidence with negative space, expressive typography as ornament, single-color accent against a dominant ground, stat blocks that look like *artifacts* not spreadsheets.
**Don't take:** Mörk Borg's screaming yellow. Our gothic is warmer, more amber.

### Old natural history plates (Maria Sibylla Merian, Ernst Haeckel)
**Take:** the engraved, line-work for creature illustrations; latinate labels; specimen-pin layouts; the implicit reverence for the subject.
**Don't take:** the literal scientific frame — we want the *feel* of an old field guide haunted by something.

## Visual principles

These are the rules a designer (or Claude) should be able to apply without re-reading anything else:

1. **Color sets mood, not meaning.** See `design-system.md` palette. Pick one accent per page based on the page's emotional weight — never assign accents to factions, regions, or threats.
2. **Texture should suggest a surface, not decorate one.** Bark grain, chitin sheen, frost lacework, spider silk — used sparingly as borders, watermarks, or under content.
3. **Bugs are real bugs.** When illustrating, reference real species. No generic fantasy bugs. A leafcutter has a leafcutter's mandibles and gait.
4. **Ornament is structural, not applied.** A spider-silk divider holds two sections apart; a fiddlehead curls at a section break because the page needs to breathe there. Ornament without a job gets cut.
5. **Type carries the gothic. Layout carries the whimsy.** A serious old-world serif over a generous, almost storybook layout is the sweet spot — reverse it and you get either a textbook or a Halloween flyer.
6. **One or two accents at a time.** A given page picks one or two accents (frost / amber / pitch / cordyceps / pollen / lichen) as its dominant, chosen by mood. Never the full rainbow.

---

## What success looks like

When a player glances at a creature card, they should feel a small chill *before* they read a word. When a DM opens an encounter sheet mid-fight, they should find the AC value in under a second. When a session recap header loads, it should read as if torn from a field journal that someone, recently, was afraid to finish writing.

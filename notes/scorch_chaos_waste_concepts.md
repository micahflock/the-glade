# The Summer Ring — Chaos Waste Concepts

> **Status: options doc, not canon.** Working response to the "magical chaos waste"
> planning memo, laid against what is already written in `memory/World_Codex.md`,
> `sessions/s06_draft.md`, `sessions/s07_draft.md`, and `sessions/s08_draft.md` §B.
> Use `/scribe` to land anything that gets picked.

---

## 1. The reframe, and what it actually fixes

The memo's pitch: the summer ring is not a heat waste, it's a **magical chaos waste**.
Still dry, hot, burnt — but the defining feature is that this close to the epicenter
the amplification has stopped being something that happens *to creatures* and started
being something that happens *to physics*. Nothing holds still. Nobody is in control.

Four specific, non-vibes reasons this beats the heat version:

**1. It closes an open question that is currently blocking.**
`Open_Questions.md` line 28: *"The ants established the shape — infiltration by
disguise, find the faction's internal fracture, and split it rather than fight it.
Does that same shape work for the summer termites and the Fall, or does it get
repetitive? Needs a second pattern before the next zone."*

A zone with no faction is *definitionally* not that pattern. There is nobody to
infiltrate, nobody to split, no queen to reach. The problem stops being social and
becomes spatial and attritional. That is the second pattern, arriving exactly when the
doc says it's needed.

**2. It fixes the patrol logic — as the memo says outright.**
"It's hot" does not stop an army; armies cross deserts. "It's unpredictable" does — you
cannot run a supply line through terrain that rewrites itself. It also makes the Red Ant
Queen look *shrewd* for turning back rather than lazy.

**3. The magic gradient finally cashes out.**
`Homebrew_Rules.md` already commits to "all magic amplified toward the epicenter" and
"what is X bug but MORE?" as the monster frame. Through winter and spring that has been
flavor plus stat bumps. Making it environmental is the first time the players will *feel*
the gradient rather than be told about it — and it earns the +3 jump to level 9.

**4. It sets up the Fall by opposition.**
Scorch: no one is in control. Fall: one thing controls everything, including the dead.
Chaos → singular, cordyceps-mediated order is a much sharper turn than desert → swamp.

### The one thing the reframe costs

The zone loses its *antagonist*. Winter had earwig bandits; spring has the alliance. A
chaos waste has weather. Environmental hazard is not a villain, and three sessions of
"the ground is weird" gets old. The fix is §5 (survivors give it a face) and §9 (the
stinger line gives it an off-screen hand). Flagged here so it isn't discovered at the table.

## 2. What the zone actually is

**The physics is unreliable, and that's the whole zone.** Not "spells go wrong" —
*ambient reality is drifting*. Three signals, in the order the party meets them:

1. **Water refuses the ground.** (Already written into `s08_draft` §B — keep it exactly
   as pitched.) The first sign is not a monster, it's a waterskin missing its mark and
   the spill beading up and rolling away like mercury.
2. **Small things are powerful.** The bottom of the food chain got the magic. An aphid —
   livestock, background, *food* for the entire campaign so far — is now a hazard.
3. **The land does not stay put.** Not earthquakes: *revision*. You come back through a
   pass you crossed yesterday and it is a different pass.

**No enclaves, no cities.** Per the memo. The only settled thing is the survivor holdfast
(§5), and it is not a town — it is people refusing to leave, in a structure built for a
hundred times their number.

**Where the horror sits.** Campaign tone statement is *"horror announces itself in
absences."* The Scorch's absence is **succession** — nothing here is anything's child.
The warped things didn't inherit their shapes, they were *assigned* them, at random,
recently. There are no families in the Scorch. That's what should make the party uneasy
before anything attacks them.

### The termite reconciliation

"No one is in control" kills the termite *faction*. It does not have to kill the mound —
the tropes list wants that dungeon and it's the natural home for the gauntlet.

**Resolution: the termites were the faction, and they lost.**

The mound is still the largest structure in the ring. It is abandoned, or worse. That
does two things at once:

- **It's your gauntlet container** — a megastructure built for millions, now holding a few
  hundred wrong things. A dungeon with a reason to be big, a reason to be full of loot,
  and a reason nobody cleaned it out.
- **It evidences the emptiness instead of asserting it.** The party doesn't get told
  nobody rules the Scorch; they walk through the ruins of the last thing that tried.

Real *Macrotermes* mounds are passive ventilation engines that self-regulate internal
temperature. So the mound is the only cool place in the ring, and it's cool because it
still **breathes**. Dungeon feature, hazard system, and horror image in one: the building
is respirating and its occupants are dead.

## 4. What is the party doing there?

Not in the memo, and it's the thing that makes or breaks a gauntlet. In the Overgrowth
they had a mandate. If the Scorch is just transit, depth has no pull and the crawl is a chore.

| Objective | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- |
| Cross it | Weakest. Pure transit. Depth is punished, not rewarded. |
| Find something in it (the Black Queen's trail, scout intel) | Fine, thin. |
| Get something *out* of it for someone else | Good — gives the survivors leverage. |
| **The way into the Fall is closed, and the only thing that opens it is deep in the Scorch** | **Recommended.** Makes the gauntlet mandatory and makes depth *mean* something. Classic, and it works. |

The Leafcutter Queen is the natural source of this — she sent scouts, and they came back
with a problem rather than a route.

## 5. The grim survivors

The memo's "grim survivors that are somehow able to protect themselves." They carry more
weight than their screen time suggests: in a ring with no faction, **the only social
scenes in the zone are enormous by default.** They're also the fix for the "no antagonist"
risk in §1.

- **Not a species — a remnant.** Mixed. Whoever was left. A texture the campaign hasn't
  used: every settlement so far has been species-coded (Canopia = pollinators,
  Weevilville = detritivores).
- **Their attitude toward the party is not hostility, it's *actuarial*.** They've watched
  people walk in before. Not impressed, not cruel — they'll trade, they'll warn you once,
  and they will not come get you. Grounded, not dreary.
- **Named candidates are in §8D.**

## 6. Structure — the gauntlet

**Current DM lean (2026-08-20): Option B, the gauntlet. No true roguelike reset.
Possible near-TPK into a mysterious rescue instead.**

### The gauntlet, as leaning

One-way. Enter here, exit there; the way back closes behind you (the wavefront shifted,
the ground revised). No long rests except in the tent, or exactly one. Every resource
comes from what's found. Most 1e-authentic, tensest, and it fits a 2-session ring without
needing a hub, a run loop, or meta-progression to justify itself.

### The near-TPK rescue — worth designing rather than improvising

The instinct is good and it's a better fit than a mechanical reset, but it only works if
it's set up in advance. Two things to decide before it can fire:

1. **Who rescues them, and were they already on screen?** A rescuer the party has never
   seen is a deus ex machina; a rescuer they walked past in the first hour is a *reveal*.
   Strongest option: the survivors from §5, or the stinger-runner from §9 — establish them
   early, have them decline to help, then have them show up anyway.
2. **What does the rescue cost?** A free save deflates the gauntlet. A rescue that puts
   them in debt, strips their gear (this is the natural place for the **planned gear-loss
   event** still open in `Open_Questions.md`), or costs the rescuer something irreversible
   keeps the stakes. The rescue should *hurt*.

The clean version: they go down, they wake up somewhere else, missing most of what they
were carrying, owing someone. That is a gauntlet beat, not a reset.

### One firm recommendation on chaos

**Do not trigger surges off the party casting spells.** If casting rolls on a chaos table,
Nancy and Drix stop casting and the zone taxes them for playing their characters. Trigger
off **the world** — round timers, damage thresholds, terrain, enemy abilities. Then chaos
is a shared hazard everyone fights *around*. Write ~20 bespoke entries that land on the
*battlefield* rather than on a person (the PHB sorcerer table is mostly personal, mostly duds).

## 7. Encounter design shapes

Six shapes rather than a monster list. Standing constraint from `s08_draft` §C: **Nancy is
very hard to pin down** (bonus-action disengage, 40 speed, high AC) and *"will fuck up the
game in an arena."* The DMs asked for interesting terrain and secondary objectives. Every
shape below answers that — and shape 2 makes her mobility an *asset* rather than a problem,
which is a better fix than nerfing her.

**1. The Attrition Wave.** Not a boss — a tide. Individually trivial, functionally endless.
The objective is never "kill them all," it's "finish the task before you're covered."
Nancy's mobility doesn't help, because the threat is *area*, not a target. Very 1e: the
wandering encounter you're supposed to run from. **This is the cleave shape.**

**2. The Terrain Is The Monster.** The antagonist is a place. A surge field, a collapsing
thermal, a floor that revises itself every round. Enemies incidental. The one where being
fast with a bonus-action disengage is the *answer* rather than the problem.

**3. The Stabilizer.** One enemy whose whole deal is that it *suppresses* the chaos in a
radius. Inside its aura, magic works normally. Outside, everything surges. Fight it on its
terms in the bubble, or drag it out into the chaos (hurts everyone, including you). A
secondary-objective fight with a real dial the players control. Best shape in the list —
see the **diving bell spider** (§8C).

**4. The Push-Your-Luck Room.** Obvious loot, escalating cost per round you stay. Either no
enemy, or one arriving on a visible clock. Pure decision tension, near-zero combat math.
Excellent pacing filler, and the most 1e thing here.

**5. The Apex.** Two bodies, one fight. **No brood on screen**, per §3.

**6. The Social One.** The danger is that the survivors might be *right* about the party
being a liability.

## 8. The bug roster

Not desert-restricted, per DM note. All real species — matching how
`creatures/magical-bugs.md` is built. These are picks to react to, not finished stat
blocks; run the keepers through `/magic-bug-generator` then `/stat-block`.

### Design note: make cleave a system, not a rule

`s08_draft` §3c establishes **Overkill** — excess damage from a killing blow carries to
another creature of the same type within 5 ft. and chains while excess remains — and the
Firecaller's temp-HP *Royal Cadence* exists to switch it off.

The mistake would be making every Scorch horde cleave identically. The roster below gives
four different relationships to Overkill, so "lots of little guys" plays differently every
time:

| Relationship | Creature | Effect at the table |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chains beautifully** | Army ants, thrips | The baseline power fantasy. Let them have it once. |
| **Chains, then punishes** | Aphids | Every death splits. Cleave *accelerates* the problem. |
| **Won't chain — spacing** | Springtails | They scatter randomly; almost never within 5 ft. of each other when one dies. |
| **Won't chain — armor** | Velvet ants | Individually tiny and nearly uncrushable. The cleave engine hits a wall and the party has to actually fight three little guys. |

---

### A. Little guys — cleave fodder

**Springtails** · *Orchesella*, *Dicyrtomina* (Collembola)
The most abundant arthropods on earth, so numbers are canon. Real mechanism: a **furcula**,
a spring-loaded tail latched under tension against the body. Release it and the springtail
is flung many body-lengths — in a **poorly controlled direction**. A mob that involuntarily
teleport-hops is the chaos waste in miniature, and it's real biology rather than invented
randomness. Globular springtails are also genuinely cute, which is the Miyazaki end of the
dial before it turns.
*Mechanically:* they relocate at the start of each round; the map is never the same twice.
Defeats cleave by geometry rather than by immunity.

**Army ants** · *Eciton burchellii*
Blind. Navigate entirely by pheromone trail. Bivouac by linking their own bodies into a
living structure. And — documented, real — when the trail loops back on itself they form a
**circular mill**: a spiral of ants marching until they die of exhaustion. In a ring where
chemical signals have been scrambled, permanent death-spirals are free set dressing that is
also a hazard, a landmark, and a horror image. Walk into a mill and it absorbs you into the
loop.
*Mechanically:* the baseline cleave horde. Also the best **Attrition Wave** (§7 shape 1).

**Aphids** · *Aphis nerii* (oleander aphid)
The memo's own image, and the one that needs no explanation at the table: the party has
known aphids as *livestock* for the entire campaign. Vivid yellow, dense mats, and
genuinely toxic (sequesters cardiac glycosides). Real biology worth stealing: aphids are
**parthenogenetic** — clonal, no males needed — and have **telescoping generations**, where
a newborn already carries her own developing daughters. Kill one and you kill three
generations. Which makes the obvious inversion right there: **every one that dies splits.**
*Mechanically:* the anti-cleave. Chaining is how you lose. Also sidesteps the mother-killing
motif for free, since there isn't a mother.

**Thrips** · Thysanoptera, "thunderflies"
Barely visible, arrive in the millions, get into everything, and feed by punching and
sucking. Less characterful than the above but the purest cleave chum if a fight just wants
volume.

**Velvet ants / "cow killers"** · *Dasymutilla*
Actually wingless wasps. Brilliant red fuzz, a sting rated for the pain, an exoskeleton so
hard entomologists struggle to *pin* them, and they **squeak** when handled. The joke writes
itself: three little guys the party cannot cleave and cannot easily crush.
*Mechanically:* the cleave wall. Best used immediately after a fight where Overkill worked
gloriously.

### B. Elites, controllers, and horrors

**Jewel wasp** · *Ampulex compressa* (emerald cockroach wasp) — **strongest pick in the list**
Metallic green, beautiful. Stings a cockroach precisely in the brain to disable its escape
reflex, then **leads it by the antenna, like a dog**, into a burrow, and lays an egg on it.
The victim walks to its own grave and does not resist. This is the Del Toro end of the dial
at full extension, and it does something structurally valuable: it prefigures the Fall's
mind-control horror **chemically rather than fungally**, so it doesn't spend the cordyceps
reveal.
*Also an NPC candidate.* A jewel wasp who is polite, and has already decided what you're for.

**Corpse-carrying assassin bug** · *Acanthaspis petax*
Real: stacks the drained husks of its victims on its own back as camouflage and armor — a
walking mound of everything it has killed. Ambush predator with a piercing rostrum and
extra-oral digestion.
*Mechanically:* the corpse-stack is ablative armor that comes off in layers, so the fight
visibly *reveals* the thing underneath. Great secondary objective.

**Ant-mimicking jumping spider** · *Myrmarachne*
Jumping spiders that mimic ants — walking on six legs and waving the front pair as fake
antennae. In a ring where identity is unstable, a thing pretending to be something else is
thematically perfect. **Nancy is a jumping spider**, which makes this personal without the
DMs having to say anything.
*Also a strong NPC.* A survivor who has lived this long by being whatever the situation
needs.

**Glowworm larvae** · *Arachnocampa*
Cave-ceiling larvae that dangle **bioluminescent silk snares** — beaded, sticky, glowing
lines hung in the dark to lure and catch flying prey. Beautiful, then horrible. The single
best-looking encounter available and a natural fit for the termite mound's upper galleries.
*Mechanically:* a ceiling hazard the party walks *into*, not a monster that comes to them.

**Velvet worm** · *Euperipatoides* (Onychophora)
Not an insect; an ancient lineage that has looked like this for ~500 million years. Hunts by
**squirting adhesive slime from two nozzles**, oscillating, in an expanding net, using up to
a quarter of its body weight per attack. Then it eats at leisure.
*Mechanically:* restraint, area denial, and a hard counter to a party that solves problems
by being fast. Note that it must *recharge* the slime — that's the window.

**Mantidfly** · Mantispidae
Raptorial mantis forelegs on a lacewing's body. Looks like two creatures fused, which is
exactly what a chaos waste should produce — and it's *real*, so the party's Nature check
comes back "this is a normal animal," which is worse. Some larvae parasitize spider egg sacs.
Natural callback after the orchid mantis.

**Ichneumon wasp** · *Megarhyssa*
An ovipositor several times its body length that **drills through solid wood** to reach
larvae tunnelling inside. Darwin cited ichneumonids as his crisis of faith.
*Mechanically:* the thing that gets through walls. Cover is not cover.

**Bolas spider** · *Mastophora*
Swings a sticky ball on a silk line — and **chemically mimics the sex pheromones of specific
moths** to call its prey to it. A hunter that lures by deception, species-specific.
*Mechanically:* it can call something *else* into the fight.

### C. Apex and set pieces

**The stabilized pair** · *Mastigoproctus giganteus* (vinegaroon) + *Centruroides sculpturatus* (bark scorpion)
The `s08` §B boss, properly grounded. The vinegaroon sprays aimed acetic acid — corrosion,
AC loss. The bark scorpion **fluoresces**, climbs, and hangs upside down from ceilings; fast
and precise where the vinegaroon is heavy. Two adults hunting as a unit. **No brood.**

**Diving bell spider** · *Argyroneta aquatica*
Builds a silk bell and fills it with air, then lives inside it underwater — the only spider
that does. Retheme the bubble: it isn't air, it's **stable physics**. A creature that carries
its own pocket of a world that still works.
*This is §7's Stabilizer, fully formed.* Fight it inside its bubble on its terms, or break the
bell and fight in the chaos alongside it.

**Giant centipede** · *Scolopendra gigantea*
A foot long. Hangs from cave ceilings and takes **bats out of the air.** Venomous, fast,
armoured, and entirely willing. Needs no magical enhancement to be a boss — which is its own
point in a zone where everything else is warped.

**Dobsonfly pair** · *Corydalus cornutus* — **best encounter-design pick in the list**
The male has enormous, absurd, sweeping mandibles that are **display equipment** — too long
to bite effectively. The female's are short, plain, and will draw blood through a glove.
A natural pair where the terrifying silhouette is nearly harmless and the plain one is the
threat. Drop this on a party that has learned to read danger by shape.

**Giant water bug** · Belostomatidae ("toe-biter")
Injects digestive enzymes and drinks its prey liquefied — it takes fish and frogs. And in
this family **the male tends the eggs**, carrying or guarding the brood. A devoted *father*
monster is the clean answer to the "we keep killing mothers" note: same protective ferocity,
opposite motif.

**Titan beetle** · *Titanus giganteus*
Among the largest insects alive; mandibles reported to snap a pencil. The adult **does not
feed at all** — it lives only to reproduce and then dies. A thing with no needs cannot be
bargained with, baited, or starved out. Pure obstacle.

**Antlion larva** · *Myrmeleon*
Pit trap in loose ground. A monster that is also a *trap* is the most 1e thing available.
The adult is a delicate lacewing-like flier, which makes a grotesque reveal if the party
ever sees one finish.

### D. NPC candidates — the survivors

Per `/magic-bug-generator`'s remit (characters, encounters, or both).

**The guide — a dung beetle** · *Scarabaeus satyrus*
Real, documented, and perfect: dung beetles **navigate by the Milky Way.** They orient to
the band of the galaxy, not to landmarks.

In a ring where the land rewrites itself, a creature that never navigated by landmarks in
the first place is **the only reliable guide in the Scorch** — and it explains, with zero
handwaving, why an army can't cross but one specialist can. He is the natural runner of the
stinger line in §9. Grim, unimpressed, charges too much, and is right to.

**The quartermaster — a harvester ant** · *Pogonomyrmex*
Obsessive seed-cacher, so the holdfast survived because somebody **had stores**. Also carries
the most painful sting in North America, so she can hold a door. The one who decides whether
the party is worth feeding.

**The water — Namib desert beetles** · *Onymacris*, *Stenocara*
Fog-basking darkling beetles that harvest water from air onto their own shells. In a ring
where water rolls off the ground, these are wells with legs. Herd animals, a livelihood, or
a person — whichever the scene needs.

**The armed one — a bombardier beetle** · *Brachinus*
Sprays a boiling, caustic chemical blast. A direct callback to Vikki in Canopia — the party
already knows what a bombardier beetle can do, which means they'll read this one correctly
and instantly.

**The whimsy — a peacock spider** · *Maratus*
Thumbnail-sized, iridescent, and performs an elaborate courtship dance with a fanned
abdomen. One small brilliant thing that survived by being too small to matter. The Miyazaki
counterweight the ring badly needs, and a natural connection point for Drix.

## 9. The connection already sitting in canon — use this

In **Session 7**, at the palace approach, the party saw carts of strange reagents: juniper
berries, glowing goop, and — **scorpion stingers.** It's in `campaign_state.yaml` as a live
thread and in `World_Codex.md` under the palace.

Scorpions are the Scorch apex. So:

> **Somebody is bringing scorpion parts out of the Scorch and selling them to the
> Red Ant Queen's lab.**

Free, already canon, already seen by the players, currently doing nothing. It gives the ring:

- **an entry NPC** — the dung beetle guide in §8D runs that line,
- **a reason the Scorch isn't sealed** despite the patrols turning back — an army can't
  cross it, but one very good, very lucky specialist can,
- **a thread tying the Session 8 heist forward** into the next ring rather than the two arcs
  sitting side by side,
- and a live question the party will *want* answered: what is the Queen building out of
  apex-predator parts?

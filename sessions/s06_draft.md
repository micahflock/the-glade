## 3. The Mantis Fight — Headline Combat Encounter

**Framing intent:** This is the session's combat set piece and the place where the party gets to flex level 6. They are **3 squishy level 6 PCs with a lot of CC**. The encounter is designed to defeat the binary "land CC and trivialize" / "miss CC and TPK" failure mode.

### 3a. Battlefield

**The map (per transcript):** A patch where the green Overgrowth mat and the original flower patch are fighting for the same ground. From the transcript:

> "A floor of the mat of green that you have to scramble over, and then flower stalks with flowers growing up out of the mat. As if the mat of green and the original flower patch were essentially fighting, trying to grow through and over each other."

> "It's a bunch of floating islands, some leaves, some flowers. We could have vines growing that they could walk across between some of them. Some of them they have to jump across."

**Movement rules established:**
- Party is **on top of the mat**, not walking through it — but the mat top is *not* flat-flat. They are hopping between leaves and flowers as discrete plateaus.
- Some plateaus are connected by **walkable vines**. Others require **jumping**. Nancy and Tabitha both have jump; Drix has limited flight.
- A flower equals roughly a **20-foot square** for combat-mechanics purposes.

### 3c. Camouflage Mechanic (mama mantis)

Transcript settled on the **flower-bound camo** version (over the alternate "illusory duplicates" pitch and over generic invisibility):

- On a single flower, the mantis can **camouflage and become unseen**. While camouflaged on a flower it cannot be targeted by ranged attacks unless a PC is in/at the same flower.
- It **cannot move between flowers in camo** — moving breaks camo, and the players see the motion. They will know which flower it just moved into, but they can't see *where on the flower* it is. ("The flower is equivalent to a 20-foot square. It could be anywhere on that 20-foot square.")
- To engage it ranged: a PC must come to the flower it's on, force it visible (melee engage / get on the flower), then it can be targeted.
- **Movement rule:** flower-to-flower hops are **adjacent-only**, so the party can track it.
- **Optional:** a Perception save/check when it moves between flowers, to add a dash of mystery about which adjacent flower it picked.

> **Why this resolves the binary-fight problem (per transcript):** *"They have to either get in on it and deal with it in melee — which none of them are particularly strong at — or they coordinate, send one person to engage in melee, CC it, hit it, and then it can move away. That lets the fight not be just a knockdown drag-out situation."*

# Beyond Session 6 — Arc Decisions Made in This Planning

## A. The Seasons Are Named

- The next zone is **the Scorch** (summer / fire / heat).
- The zone after that is **the Fall** (was previously unnamed; now confirmed: *"I want to call it the Fall."* *"The Fall? Sure. Oh, that makes sense."*)
- The Rot is the final zone with the big bad. *"An entirely zombified city in the Rot."*

## B. Big Beats of the Remaining Campaign

1. **Combined colony (Overgrowth):** Heist-style infiltration of the Red Ant Queen's flower-petal palace to reach the **Leafcutter Queen** in her private quarters. She gives them story info + a fungus-magic reward. *No full rebellion arc* — explicitly out of scope.
2. **The Scorch:** another city, *possibly moved underground because the surface is too hot.*
3. **The Rot:** zombified city, final big bad encounter.

## C. The Red Ant Queen's Palace — Setting Bible

- The Red Ant hive is **above ground**, a **palace built of ever-living, never-rotting flower petals** (built for the Red Ant Queen by the leafcutters using flowers that don't decay because of the Bloom).
- The Red Ant Queen is a megalomaniac running the zone. She throws constant **big-ass fancy balls**: bards from everywhere, pit fights with betting, debauchery. Non-ant guests are normal — bees, wasps, butterflies, moths.
- The hive contains **on-site bee facilities** (bees making honey inside one of the wings/arms of the palace) — *"That would be a really cool area to cut through."*
- Standard megalomaniacal-king-feast tropes are explicitly invited.

## D. The Heist Plan (high level)

- **Public phase:** Party is invited as dignitaries via the weevil channel (Gleam). They can move freely as guests in public areas for some period of time.
- **Stealth phase:** The Leafcutter Queen is paraded briefly (forced — she's reluctant and obsessed with her fungus work) then escorted away by fire ants. Reaching her means leaving the public areas and infiltrating the high-security wing.
- **Approach options:** full sneak; or use **Drix as a traveling bard** to enter publicly, **Nancy dancing** ("Nancy dance-y"), and **Tabby** sneaking in from the outside.
- **Fail states are escalating, not binary:** no alarm switch — discovery summons investigators. Party can quietly dispatch the first 1–2 and melt back into shadows. If that fails, escalation forces them to run.

## E. Specialist / Flashback Mechanic (Gleam's offer)

- Gleam presents **four specialist NPCs**; party picks **two** to take on the heist as off-screen support.
- Each specialist has a **theme** (e.g. "fighter who can hide" — Rocky; "good at making disturbances" — Sassy; "good at putting things to sleep" — Sleepy). Specifically *not* a fixed list of abilities.
- During the heist, when something goes wrong, the players invoke a flashback: *"Earlier, [specialist] did X to set this up."* Players invent the flavor; DM either accepts or gently nudges toward a creative answer that fits the specialist's theme.
- DMs are **not** playing these NPCs at the table. They are flashback levers.

## F. Leafcutter Queen — Story / Lore

- The alliance with the Red Ant Queen was **the Leafcutter Queen's idea**, not the Red Queen's. She noticed leaves stopping degrading, started raiding the winter zone alone for biological matter, lost too many ants doing it, and pulled in the Red Queen *thinking she could keep her under control.* It immediately spiraled — but immediately also worked. So she decided this was just how things were going to be.
- She is obsessed with **fungi that can grow on / consume living matter** (necessitated because dead matter doesn't decay in the Bloom). Current results are imperfect — *"when you eat them, they eat you."*
- She knows **a coven member broke the pact** and caused this. [MORE DETAIL: more details about what the pact was, what breaking it did. Transcript flags this as a TBD.]
- She knows the **Red Ant Queen did NOT** break the pact — *"The Red Ant Queen isn't even smart enough to have broken the pact."* The Red Queen was just always on the edge; this made her insane and powerful.
- She knows / strongly suspects **the Black Ant Queen** (working name) broke the pact. She has no idea what has happened to the Black Ant Queen.
- [MORE DETAIL: confirm "Black Ant Queen" vs "ebony black ant" / "sugar ant" — transcript explicitly leaves the species open. Hook: Sven (the ebony black ant from Chitinholm) may have been in connection with his queen and gone solo when the pact broke. Optional thread.]
- The **gimmick for the broken-pact Queen:** she is now *part-lich* — the **cordyceps queen.**
- **Story outcome of the heist:** Leafcutter Queen gets *hope* — that someone is out there trying to change things — and the party gets *information* about the broken pact and the cordyceps queen.

## G. Fungus-Powered Weapon Rewards (from the Leafcutter Queen)

**Design constraints landed in transcript:**
- Each PC gets a **+1 weapon** flavored as fungus-enhanced (level-6 balance baseline).
- The fungus is one she has actually been able to grow: *fungi that feed on living flesh.*
- The +1 is the floor. On top of that, each weapon has an **awakening effect** that triggers *the first time it draws blood/ichor in a fight* — the fungus needs to feed to wake up — and the effect lasts for **the duration of that combat.** Transcript phrasing: *"the dagger gets fed and then it's hungry for a bit."*

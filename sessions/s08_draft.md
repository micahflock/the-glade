## 1. The Night — Framing, Clock, and the Intended Line

### 1a. Entry premise & the happy path

**Entry premise (carried from S07).** The party is the band's fourth act. They are booked to play; they arrive as guests/performers and can move freely in the public areas before their set.

**Setup:** The party is *"the fourth band going on… the opener for the middle set."* Structure: three bands, a break, then their set. **They arrive while earlier bands are performing, before the break** — the party is already going when they get there. The DMs left open whether the players pick their arrival time or get railroaded to a set time: *"it doesn't matter too much. Just knowing that they have time to do stuff before they have to go on is the important part."*

**Getting to backstage:** They are **not** handed a contact. The fire-ant security *"would definitely not be thinking far enough in advance"* to guide them — *but if asked* would say *"oh, okay, I can show you, I can tell you."* So the party is let loose and must think to ask to be taken backstage, or stumble onto it.

**The normal-musician itinerary (any order)** — the chill version that doesn't involve a heist, as the baseline the party deviates from:

- Green room / prep — makeup, "green room stuff." (Location: **off the stage in each ballroom, reached through the single worker's aperture behind the bar** — the same door that continues down to the kitchen. See §4a/§4b.) [MORE DETAIL: the DMs waved off pre-performance equipment setup — *"they don't have any prepared, maybe we skip that."*]
- Schmooze on the floor (before or after the green room).
- **Perform their set.**
- Get food.
- Gamble (casino).
- Pheromone lounge.
- **Attend the Red Ant Queen's coronation.**
- Party the night, leave whenever.

### 1b. If they miss their set

Not treated as an instant infiltration flag. The ants assume artist vices: *"they probably went out and got themselves high on pheromones or drunk or something."*

- If they're not in the green room shortly before their set, **someone is sent to look for them** — a "make things flow smoothly" courtesy, not a manhunt. If they're chilling in the pheromone lounge or casino, a finder locates them.
- A no-show gets a replacement pressured back on: *"you need to perform again… we'll give you more money"* — the Queen's minions make it happen.
- **Soldier ants go to higher alert** but do not assume infiltration. *"It would have to be something unlucky to really get consequences from no-showing."*
- If they've already gotten deep into the core when they miss the set, *"I don't even know if it would matter. It might make the exfil harder, but probably not."* No big impact on this mission (possible later consequences with the Red Queen, who *"doesn't necessarily know them"*).

### 1c. The coronation clock — the one scheduled beat

Everything else at the fête is player-driven (§1f). This is the exception, and it is the hinge of the night.

1. **Feast** — Great Hall set with long tables (§4c).
2. **Changeover** — staff clear the food and move the tables to the back storeroom. Visible, busy, and it signals the coronation is imminent: *"they could use the busyness to sneak back."*
3. **Both queens present.** *"At the red ant's coronation, the leaf cutter queen will be in attendance."*
4. **The Leafcutter Queen is escorted away before the ceremony begins** — *"both queens are initially at the coronation, but before the coronation starts, the leaf cutter queen is escorted away."* The party does not know this in advance: *"they don't know the queen's gonna come out and be taken away again, they don't have all that information."*
5. **Ceremony in the Great Hall** — *"The coronation venue is going to be the Great Hall after a changeover, and then the ceremony will proceed into the throne room."*
6. **Procession up the grand stair into the Throne Room**, where the queens take the thrones.

**Pressure point:** *"if they in some way prevent the leaf cutter queen from being available for the coronation,"* that is a moment their activity **might be noticed** — *"someone will come looking for the leaf cutter queen before the coronation."* Only bites if they act **before** the coronation.

### 1d. The intended solution path (the "right answer")

The DMs named the optimal line explicitly. It runs on intel, not on stealth rolls:

1. **Work the room for intel about the coronation** — *"pulling that information out of the various patrons and contacts… that's a successful way of doing it."* This is what the operatives in §7 are for.
2. **Have a distraction staged for the moment the ceremony starts** — *"setting it up so that when the coronation is going on, they have a distraction ready to get into the back rooms and get to the core while a coronation is happening, is like the quote-unquote right answer to solve this."*
3. **Move to the core while the hall is watching the crown.**

**If they blow it.** *"They'll do something like that, or some worse version of that, and some amount of guards and shit will hit the fan."* Failure tolerance is high — *"absolute worst case, I think they should be able to just fight their way through this,"* as long as they don't do *"something aggressively stupid."* The design intent: *"the goal is going to be for them to, like, almost get discovered a few times, and they can pull this stuff out to undo stuff."* Governing principle — *"we want it to be fun. We don't want it to be crushingly realistic."*

**The one hard no.** *"They shouldn't be able to fight their way through the throne room to the back. That shouldn't be a straight fight."* Fighting up the grand stair into a packed throne room including the Red Queen — *"they will die from that."* But starting that fight and then breaking off is fine: *"they can start that fight, run to the kitchens another way and get into the core still, even."*

### 1e. Beeatrix — the liability who follows them around

- **She does not know they are infiltrating.** *"She doesn't know that they're infiltrating, right? As far as she understands."* — *"No, yeah, for sure, for sure."*
- **No stat block, but she can die.** *"She doesn't need to, like, have a stat block and be a fighter"* — and yet *"she absolutely can get killed in a fight."* Considered and rejected: the invulnerable-escort model — *"kind of like… Ashley from Resident Evil? Like, she cowers and she can't really get killed?"*
- **In a fight she screams and runs.** *"If a fight breaks out, I think she screams. And runs away"* — *"depending on how drunk she is."* She is not an idiot about it: *"she'll go hide behind a table and shit… she'll go and she'll try and save herself."* Note the interaction with §3: **her screaming is a noise event.** A fight Beeatrix witnesses cannot be a quiet fight.
- **Never an asset.** *"She should be an active liability pretty much throughout this whole process."* / *"We should use her not as an advantage to them in any way, shape, or form unless it is really dead obvious."* Even a clever play by the party should land badly: *"even if they do something very clever, I think she should… fuck it up somehow. Think Oblivion NPC."*
- **Tasking her fails, scaled to the task.** *"If they give her a task, I think she should fuck it up"* — *"depending on the task. If it's ghosting in a corner, I think she can manage that."*
- **The one sanctioned use:** *"they can make a distraction out of her."*

### 1f. Disruption is the party's job, not the schedule's

The fête is **a normal party** — *"the players can cause all the disruption they want, otherwise it's just a nice party."* Events are **not predetermined / not a Deathloop-style clockwork** (*"that's a level of effort that goes above and beyond"* — *"we're doing Deathloop minus the loops"*).

*"I don't think we need that many concrete room events."* The night's structure already carries itself — *"if they hang out long enough, the Great Hall starts getting cleared, and then there's the coronation"* — and the party supplies the rest. The Rube Goldberg fantasy was named and set aside: *"in my head I'm like they should be able to set up this Rube Goldberg contraption of events… but that's just ridiculous. I mean, they could, but they've got to come up with them."*

**The dynamic to keep live:** *"a fight could start in the casino"* → that *"might pull guards from a nearby room for a moment"* → **opening** for the party to slip down another path. Disruption cuts both ways — it makes a room harder, or it makes a route open. Every room's **Levers** line in §4 is written for this.

**Access reminder that shapes everything:** the outward-facing rooms ring the outside, so *"they can't really go around the outside into the core."* The only ways in are **the Great Hall (→ Throne Room), the Kitchen, or the Pheromone Lounge storeroom.**

## 2. The Palace — Shared Texture and the Map

### 2a. Aesthetics & structure (applies to every outward-facing room)

- **Material.** Rooms are built from flower petals treated as panels — *"each of the petals are almost like panels, and they're attached on the edges."* Each room leans on a different flower and color. The in-world equivalent of "gold and marble" is *"marigold petals and white rose petals."*
- **Structure.** Modeled on German/Amish **wax-cut candles** — *"where they cut into it and fold it back down and expose the colors"* — read as *"natural flying buttresses,"* an *"almost overwhelming combination of color and structure, but still kind of organic-looking,"* built from *"different colored petals of different shades, overlapping."* Vaulted, filigreed ceilings. On the inside you see *"the inverted side of that… a two-dimensional sheet instead of a three-dimensional thing."* DM plan: *"describe it to them as this and then show them the picture of the candles."*
- **Layout.** The rooms don't touch. *"Each of these room structures hangs alone, and it's connected to other stuff by flying buttresses and walkways"* — *"an open-air walkway between the two,"* some enclosed, with roofs that are *"a roof for aesthetics only."* Reference: Lothlórien houses *"wrapped around trees connected by open walkways."*
- **Design philosophy (security).** *"They weren't really thinking about security. They were thinking about what seemed the most decadent."* This is why the soft routes between rooms are the bars and service/"help" tunnels.
- **Orientation.** Facing the Ant Queens from outside: **Ballroom 1 (pink) is on the left, Ballroom 2 (purple) is on the right, and the Great Hall sits between them.**
- **Lighting.** The palace is *"mostly lit by torches"* — and the back rooms are lit too, by **glowworm lanterns**: *"you slap them and the glowworms go."* Not crucified to the wall — *"they're just like, in a container, like a jiggly sack that you just slap."* (Rejected alternative: live glowworms pinned to the wall — *"that's way less fucked up than what I suggested."*) Staff **can turn them off** — see §3f.
- **Bore-able petal vs. bark/leaf faces — an in-session call.** Not pre-marked on a map: *"I think it's an in-session call."* Rule of thumb: outward-facing petal panels are soft, the core is armored.
- **The palace is flammable.** Dried petal panels, torches, and a guard corps that spits fire. Fire is the party's biggest available distraction and the fastest way to lose control of the night.

## 3. Security — Guards, Alert Ladder, and the Roster

### 3a. Guard economy — do not track a fixed roster

The prior draft's *"like 16 guards, plus the red ant queen"* is superseded, and so is the reconciliation against S07's counts. The ruling: *"there's an infinite number of them."* — *"It makes more sense for us to essentially infinitely spawn guards. Make it up on the fly."* What gets designed instead is **how many show up where**, i.e. *"decide how many guards are going to show up in a batch in different areas… maybe think about it in terms of alert levels."* Standing placement per room is in each §4 entry; the ladder is below.

**The two tiers.** The ants posted around the fête are **not the Queen's elite** — they are decoration with weapons on. The elite exist, they are a separate and much nastier thing, and they only come out when the palace decides something is actually wrong.

### 3b. The alert ladder

| Level | What tripped it | What arrives |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **0 — Fête** | Nothing. Baseline all night. | Standing placement only (per room, §4). Nobody is looking for anything. |
| **1 — Noticed** | A guard sprays Alarm Musk; a fight is heard; Beeatrix screams; someone reports a missing act or a missing queen. | **4–6 Red Ant Guards** converge on the noise. *"more than two guards show up to that. Like four to six guards show up,"* with *"more that'll come running if they start some shit."* Still no royals. |
| **2 — Squad** | *Enough noise, or something specifically suspicious.* A won fight still counts — *"I think them fighting is loud."* Bodies found. An intruder seen in a back corridor. | **A royal squad is dispatched: minimum 2 (1 Firecaller + 1 Bulwark), up to 4 (1 Firecaller + 3 Bulwarks)** — plus whatever ordinary guards are already on the noise. See §3e. |
| **3 — Wildly out of hand** | Fire, an open running fight in a public room, the coronation disrupted. | Red Ant Guards **in fairly large numbers** — 8, 12, as many as the scene wants; this is where the cleave rule earns its keep. A second royal squad. |
| **4 — Lockdown** | Guards start disappearing and it's noticed. | Everything goes to high alert and **the rest cluster around the Red Ant Queen** — which *"maybe gives them an opening to escape."* |

**Patrol behavior — DECIDED (was open).** Back-room patrols **stand and fight while making noise**; they do not disengage to fetch help. Mechanically that's the Red Ant Guard's *Alarm Musk* — the first damage it takes broadcasts its position and the direction of the threat to every red ant within 60 ft. **The corollary is the whole stealth game: a patrol dropped before it takes damage dies quiet.** *"if they can get the jump on them, they can take them out in a surprise round, then they're good"* — *"before they can call for help."* The party *"should be able to obliterate them in one round. At least one of them."* Patrol pairs are deliberately sized *"one less than their party."* (The alternative the DMs weighed and set aside — *"they're gonna prioritize calling for help, like they'll disengage"* vs. *"are they fire ants and they're fucking ready to go?"* — resolves to the second, but the *"only one round"* of grace they wanted the party to have survives intact: Alarm Musk fires on first damage, not on first sighting.)
[SLOP CHECK: the DM note reads *"stand and fight while making noise and fire"* — "fire" is read here as the guards' fire attacks (the Ember Lance), not a separate signal-fire mechanic. Confirm.]

**The rampage ceiling.** *"if they really want to go and kill everything,"* there is a point at which they can clear the field — *"but I doubt that'll come up."* They *could* kill the Red Ant Queen but *"it's not really the point"*; she'd be *"really, really strong,"* and doing it for real would take a whole separate plot (coalition, poison, etc.).

### 3c. Overkill (Cleave) — house rule for this session

Written for the ordinary guards so that a swarm reads as a swarm and not as a slog.

> **Overkill.** When a single attack reduces a **Red Ant Guard** to 0 hit points, damage in excess of its remaining hit points carries over to one other Red Ant Guard within 5 ft. of it, and can chain onward the same way while excess remains.
>
> - Applies to **Red Ant Guards only.** Overkill never carries to or from a Royal Ant Bulwark, a Royal Ant Firecaller, or any non-ant creature (the royals' *Royal Chitin* trait says so explicitly).
> - **Temporary hit points stop the chain.** The Firecaller's *Royal Cadence* exists to shut cleave off — excess is absorbed, not passed. Killing the caster turns cleave back on.
> - [SLOP CHECK: written to apply to any single-target attack, melee or ranged, but **not** to area spells (which already hit everyone in the area). Confirm — the note says *"party members will be able to apply overkill damage to adjacent ant guards"* without restricting the attack type.]

### 3f. The Gloomstalker problem

*"I am concerned about the Gloomstalker. Encountering a series of guard patrols is gonna be very easy pickings — especially because this is at night. They're gonna be in hallways. I believe that Tabby is fucking invisible in the dark."*

Mitigation as it now stands: the back rooms **are lit** by glowworm lanterns (§2a), so Tabby is not free-invisible by default — but staff can douse them, and so can the party, which hands the advantage back deliberately rather than by accident. ## 5. Exfiltration

*"exfiltration is something they need to think about."* Two states, which the party can transition between (e.g. get alerted in a ballroom, set a fire, get lost in the scramble, then slip out):

- **Stealth state** — undetected, sneaking out.
- **Alert state** — being chased; often forces a stand-and-fight.

### 5a. Caught in a queen's room

If found in the room with the Red Ant Queen or the Leafcutter Queen: they can **attempt to eliminate the guard**; *"if they don't, then they're pretty screwed."* The **Red Ant Queen has a secret exit**; the **Leafcutter Queen does not** — *"I don't think she has an escape plan… you have to have hope"* (she wouldn't use one even if she had it).

### 5b. The three exits

*"The only exits are the secret path, getting out via flying, or the main entrance."*

1. **The secret path** — Red Ant Queen's Chambers → secret door → onto the Spiral Ramp → down to the leafcutter highway. Skips the outward-facing castle entirely. The party may not know it exists.
2. **Flying** — off any landing pad (Ballroom 1, Ballroom 2, Great Hall). Two flavors:
   - **Pre-arranged butterfly** — as performers, convince a butterfly on standby (*"someone else hired a butterfly, he's sitting on the platform… we just finished our gig, take us back to the city for an extra"*). He'll haggle but do it.
   - **Tackle a butterfly off the ledge** — *"book it off the ramp and tackle a butterfly off the edge"* on a good roll; the butterfly flaps to not die and can't stop the fall. (DM riff: *"What the fuck, dude — you're pulling us both down!"*) Bugs don't take real fall damage in-fiction, *"but this is D&D and we make the rules."*
3. **The main entrance** — the Spiral Ramp, i.e. the servants'/ground-bug entrance up from the leafcutter highway (fortified servants' gate w/ watchtower per S07). From the core you can go straight down the ramp.

**Fall-damage ruling.** An unassisted jump is not survivable-by-default. Normal 5e fall damage applies, *"but it's not just the fall damage — it's falling into the Overgrowth"* below: *"if you jump from the castle into the tree canopy it's still not gonna end well… it might be fatal."* The palace reads as *"essentially a castle on a cliff… it is up."* **Requirement: to go off an edge on purpose they must have some way to slow or guide the fall** — *"they've gotta have some way to slow or guide their fall. For sure."* (The butterfly, a makeshift parachute, or a spell.) DM read on likelihood: *"I don't think the players are just gonna fuckin' send it off the edge. That doesn't sound like something they would do."*

**Improvised outs raised:** set a fire / create a big distraction / blow a hole, then get lost in the scramble; a **makeshift parachute** or **hostage butterfly** off a balcony.

## 6. The Leafcutter Queen — the Payoff Scene

The DMs flagged this as the open worry: *"I'm a little worried about their narrative payoff still… it's not a reward I'm concerned about, it's why are they going to believe her."* What they landed on:

- **She gets hope / is inspired to fight back.** *"You're energizing her to actively start to fight back, as opposed to having accepted defeat… we are now straight up inspiring the rebellion."* The rebellion is secondary and *"nothing comes of it yet"* — but *"it changes how she feels about the future substantially."* This is the intended payoff.
- **She gives the party information** — the real player-facing reward: details on *"the coven magic being broken,"* the warped queens, and a pointed hint at the culprit. *"For the players, it's actually quite valuable for them to know more of the mechanics of what happened… they're learning more of the story we're writing."*
- **The Fungus Rune** they already carry is expected to help her trust them. [MORE DETAIL: how the rune reads to her.]

**Scene delivery — ad-lib it.** *"That's the impassioned escape [speech]. I think that can be ad-libbed."* — *"Yeah, absolutely."* No scripted read-aloud needed; the material below is enough to run her cold.

### 6a. Where her head is

Not despair — resignation plus tunnel vision. *"She's not necessarily lost hope, but… she's given up fighting the Red Queen and has sort of transitioned to only focusing on her fungus, and her fungus is all that she cares about now."* / *"How she's feeling, which is hopeless, and sort of focusing on making the best of the situation."*

# Background — Arc Material (not session beats)

## A. The Brood Coven & the Forbidden Magic — DECIDED

The DMs reconstructed and then settled the campaign's central backstory. They flagged it as the thing they'd been circling for a while: *"that's what was dark. That's what we earned, is that explanation. That nugget."* This is what the Leafcutter Queen can reveal in §6.

### The Brood Coven

- **The name is chosen.** *"The paradigm of a witch coven isn't too much to use. It doesn't have to be a blood coven. We could call it a **brood coven**."* — *"I love brood coven. I love that name."*
- **What it is:** the ant queens *"are more powerful than other insects because they are whole hives unto themselves."* The coven bonds them: *"they are, by some magical property, bonded."* Practically, *"they each got a little bit — could borrow a little bit of magic power from one another."*
- **The upside was real, and mutual:** *"they had kind of all agreed that this sharing meant that they could all prosper together."* *"They lived in a balance."*
- **The downside only surfaced later:** *"they were all linked… their fate is now tied together."*
- **Crucially, the pact is not the magic.** *"The pact isn't the magic. The Black Queen did some bad magic, and it rippled down the pact."* The pact's own term was a prohibition: *"a pact to not delve too deep into the magic."*

### The forbidden magic is TIME MAGIC

> *"I think we could just say that all time magic is forbidden and evil, because nature is all about the passage of time."* — *"Yes, dude. That's it. I love that."*

### What the Black Queen actually did

- She *"was attacked by a wolf spider infected by cordyceps, got bitten, and froze time so she didn't get infected, and her whole colony didn't fall."*
- **It overshot.** *"Rather than just what she wanted to do — which was pause the progress of the infection in her — instead it paused the world in these expanding waves."*
- **Two consequences:** the calamity itself, and *"it also succeeded in pausing the infection, which lets her control Cordyceps"* — i.e. *"she is super powerful and can control cordyceps-infected stuff."* This is the in-world reason cordyceps enemies exist at all: *"my problem with it originally was it gave no motivation for them to have any cordyceps things to fight, but that does give us that. So yeah, I'm good with that."*
- **Her motive was survival, not conquest.** *"The goal, in my head, the goal was not so much a power grab as an attempt to survive."* The DMs deliberately steered away from a straightforwardly evil version: *"I kind of like having the big bad not be just evil."*
- **The madness came after.** *"When they break the pact, they go a little crazy"* — *"the act of trying to save herself and a colony also kind of drives her crazy."*

### The ripple through the coven

*"Because they were all bonded by the brood coven, they all suffered consequences. And they all went a little crazy and got more powerful."* This is the single mechanism behind every warped queen — the Red Ant Queen's fire-and-vanity mania included (§6c).

### Endgame shape (as currently understood)

- The Black Queen is defeatable — *"an evil person who you can defeat and just turn everything off"* — **or** persuadable: *"maybe they can somehow convince her to undo it."*
- **The real big bad is the cordyceps wolf spider.** *"There still should be a big bad, so… maybe there's this big cordyceps spider."*

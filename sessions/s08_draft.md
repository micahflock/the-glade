### Palace aesthetics & structure (shared)

The room-texture transcript established a shared visual language that applies to every outward-facing room (per-room specifics are in §6):

- **Material.** Rooms are built from flower petals treated as panels — *"each of the petals are almost like panels, and they're attached on the edges."* Each room leans on a different flower and color. The in-world equivalent of "gold and marble" is *"marigold petals and white rose petals."*
- **Structure.** Modeled on German/Amish **wax-cut candles** — *"where they cut into it and fold it back down and expose the colors"* — read as *"natural flying buttresses,"* an *"almost overwhelming combination of color and structure, but still kind of organic-looking,"* built from *"different colored petals of different shades, overlapping."* Vaulted, filigreed ceilings. On the inside you see *"the inverted side of that… a two-dimensional sheet instead of a three-dimensional thing."* DM plan: *"describe it to them as this and then show them the picture of the candles."*
- **Layout.** The rooms don't touch. *"Each of these room structures hangs alone, and it's connected to other stuff by flying buttresses and walkways"* — *"an open-air walkway between the two,"* some enclosed, with roofs that are *"a roof for aesthetics only."* Reference: Lothlórien houses *"wrapped around trees connected by open walkways."*
- **Design philosophy (security).** *"They weren't really thinking about security. They were thinking about what seemed the most decadent."* This is why the soft routes between rooms are the bars and service/"help" tunnels.
- **Orientation.** Facing the Ant Queens from outside: **Ballroom 1 (pink) is on the left, Ballroom 2 (purple) is on the right, and the Great Hall sits between them.**
- **Lighting (new this pass).** The palace is *"mostly lit by torches"* — and the back rooms are lit too, by **glowworm lanterns**: *"you slap them and the glowworms go."* Not crucified to the wall — *"they're just like, in a container, like a jiggly sack that you just slap."* (Rejected alternative: live glowworms pinned to the wall — *"that's way less fucked up than what I suggested."*) Staff **can turn them off**, which matters for the darkness discussion in §4c.

## 2. The Happy Path — What a Normal Performer Does

The DMs wanted the "chill version that doesn't involve a heist" spelled out first, as the baseline the party deviates from.

**Setup:** The party is *"the fourth band going on… the opener for the middle set."* Structure: three bands, a break, then their set. **They arrive while earlier bands are performing, before the break** — the party is already going when they get there. The DMs left open whether the players pick their arrival time or get railroaded to a set time: *"it doesn't matter too much. Just knowing that they have time to do stuff before they have to go on is the important part."*

**Getting to backstage:** They are **not** handed a contact. The fire-ant security *"would definitely not be thinking far enough in advance"* to guide them — *but if asked* would say *"oh, okay, I can show you, I can tell you."* So the party is let loose and must think to ask to be taken backstage, or stumble onto it.

**The normal-musician itinerary (any order):**

- Green room / prep — makeup, "green room stuff." (Location now set: **off the stage in each ballroom, reached through the single worker's aperture behind the bar** — the same door that continues down to the kitchen. See §1 and the Ballroom entries in §6.) [MORE DETAIL: the DMs waved off pre-performance equipment setup — *"they don't have any prepared, maybe we skip that."*]
- Schmooze on the floor (before or after the green room).
- **Perform their set.**
- Get food.
- Gamble (casino).
- Pheromone lounge.
- **Attend the Red Ant Queen's coronation.**
- Party the night, leave whenever.

### 2a. If they miss their set

Not treated as an instant infiltration flag. The ants assume artist vices: *"they probably went out and got themselves high on pheromones or drunk or something."* Behavior:

- If they're not in the green room shortly before their set, **someone is sent to look for them** — as a "make things flow smoothly" courtesy, not a manhunt. If they're chilling in the pheromone lounge or casino, a finder locates them.
- A no-show gets a replacement pressured back on: *"you need to perform again… we'll give you more money"* — the Queen's minions make it happen.
- **Soldier ants go to higher alert** but do not assume infiltration. *"It would have to be something unlucky to really get consequences from no-showing."*
- If they've already gotten deep into the core when they miss the set, *"I don't even know if it would matter. It might make the exfil harder, but probably not."* No big impact on this mission (possible later consequences with the Red Queen, who *"doesn't necessarily know them"*).

## 3. The Coronation & the Leafcutter Queen

### 3a. The Leafcutter Queen is present

*"At the red ant's coronation, the leaf cutter queen will be in attendance."* This is a pressure point: *"if they in some way prevent the leaf cutter queen from being available for the coronation,"* that is a moment their activity **might be noticed** — *"someone will come looking for the leaf cutter queen before the coronation."* (Only bites if they act **before** the coronation.)

**Venue — DECIDED.** *"The coronation venue is going to be the Great Hall after a changeover, and then the ceremony will proceed into the throne room."* So: feast → staff clear the hall (§5) → ceremony in the Great Hall → procession up the grand stair into the Throne Room, where the queens take the thrones.

**Timing — NEW, and this is the hinge of the night.** Both queens are present at the start, **but the Leafcutter Queen is escorted away before the coronation actually begins**: *"both queens are initially at the coronation, but before the coronation starts, the leaf cutter queen is escorted away."* The party does not know this in advance — *"they don't know the queen's gonna come out and be taken away again, they don't have all that information."*

### 3a-i. The intended solution path (the "right answer")

The DMs named the optimal line explicitly. It runs on intel, not on stealth rolls:

1. **Work the room for intel about the coronation** — *"pulling that information out of the various patrons and contacts… that's a successful way of doing it."* This is what the operatives in §6 are for.
2. **Have a distraction staged for the moment the ceremony starts** — *"setting it up so that when the coronation is going on, they have a distraction ready to get into the back rooms and get to the core while a coronation is happening, is like the quote-unquote right answer to solve this."*
3. **Move to the core while the hall is watching the crown.**

**If they blow it.** *"They'll do something like that, or some worse version of that, and some amount of guards and shit will hit the fan."* Failure tolerance is high — *"absolute worst case, I think they should be able to just fight their way through this,"* as long as they don't do *"something aggressively stupid."* The design intent: *"the goal is going to be for them to, like, almost get discovered a few times, and they can pull this stuff out to undo stuff."* Governing principle — *"we want it to be fun. We don't want it to be crushingly realistic."*

**The one hard no.** *"They shouldn't be able to fight their way through the throne room to the back. That shouldn't be a straight fight."* Fighting up the grand stair into a packed throne room including the Red Queen — *"they will die from that."* But starting that fight and then breaking off is fine: *"they can start that fight, run to the kitchens another way and get into the core still, even."*

### 3b. Narrative payoff — the reason to reach her

The DMs flagged this as the open worry: *"I'm a little worried about their narrative payoff still… it's not a reward I'm concerned about, it's why are they going to believe her."* What they landed on:

- **She gets hope / is inspired to fight back.** *"You're energizing her to actively start to fight back, as opposed to having accepted defeat… we are now straight up inspiring the rebellion."* The rebellion is secondary and *"nothing comes of it yet"* — but *"it changes how she feels about the future substantially."* This is the intended payoff.
- **She gives the party information** — the real player-facing reward: details on *"the coven magic being broken,"* the warped queens, and a pointed hint at the culprit. *"For the players, it's actually quite valuable for them to know more of the mechanics of what happened… they're learning more of the story we're writing."*
- **The Fungus Rune** they already carry is expected to help her trust them / help them here. [MORE DETAIL: how the rune reads to her.]

**Scene delivery — RESOLVED: ad-lib it.** *"That's the impassioned escape [speech]. I think that can be ad-libbed."* — *"Yeah, absolutely."* No scripted read-aloud is needed; the DMs know the beats and the material below is enough to run her cold.

#### Where her head is

Not despair — resignation plus tunnel vision. *"She's not necessarily lost hope, but… she's given up fighting the Red Queen and has sort of transitioned to only focusing on her fungus, and her fungus is all that she cares about now."* / *"How she's feeling, which is hopeless, and sort of focusing on making the best of the situation."*

She is **not self-aware about her own warping** — play it as blind spot, not confession: *"I don't know if she needs to be that self-aware… she's like, 'you know, all I can think about these days is—'… 'I haven't suffered as much as her.'"* — *"and then, meanwhile, she's surrounded by hundreds of thousands of jars of fungus."*

#### What she and the party already share (no new reveal)

The known ground both sides can reference: they can't grow their fungus; a lot of detritivores are unable to eat; she tried to go get the bodies to feed them, but they started dying; under pressure from the Red Ant Queen she decided to work together; the arrangement runs now, but *"she doesn't feel great about using all these bodies to power it, and also knows that it's not gonna last forever."* Hence the lab: *"she's been really frantically trying to figure out a solution with the fungus to feed everyone that can't eat nectar."*

#### What she reveals (the info-dump)

- **The event, in her words:** *"One of the queens, one of the queens accessed forbidden magic, and a shockwave went through the coven and through the world. And, you know, as our powers awakened, our minds fled."* (Alternate line the DMs liked: *"our minds shuddered."*)
- **The warping of the others** — e.g. the Red Ant Queen: *"before, she was prone to theatrics, but now she's, like, insane and obsessed with fire and vanity."*
- **The forbidden magic itself** — the brood coven and the ban on time magic. Full lore in **Background §A**.

#### Naming the culprit — how she gets there

She **knows it wasn't the Red Ant Queen**, but only softly: *"she thinks that Red Ant Queen might have done something by accident, but definitely not on purpose."* Her reasoning, if pressed: *"The Red Queen is not smart enough to figure out how to break the coven. She obviously has all this fire, literal firepower now… so it could have been her, I guess, to get this firepower. But it just seems more like this fell into her hands, not that she was planning this."*

**The turn happens when the party tells her which direction it came from.** Then *"all question of the Red Ant Queen having done something has evaporated"* and she names the Black Queen herself. Verbatim, as the DMs played it:

> *"Right, it must be her. She was — I shudder to think, she was the wisest of us all. I wonder what could have caused her to do this. But it must have been her. She was the only one powerful enough to wreak this much havoc."*

## 4. Exfiltration

The DMs walked every exit because *"exfiltration is something they need to think about."* Two states, which the party can transition between (e.g. get alerted in a ballroom, set a fire, get lost in the scramble, then slip out):

- **Stealth state** — undetected, sneaking out.
- **Alert state** — being chased; often forces a stand-and-fight.

### 4a. Caught in a queen's room

If found in the room with the Red Ant Queen or the Leafcutter Queen: they can **attempt to eliminate the guard**; *"if they don't, then they're pretty screwed."*

- The **Red Ant Queen has a secret exit**; the **Leafcutter Queen does not** — *"I don't think she has an escape plan… you have to have hope"* (she wouldn't use one even if she had it).

### 4b. The exits (enumerated)

*"The only exits are the secret path, getting out via flying, or the main entrance."*

1. **The secret path** — Red Ant Queen's Chambers → secret door → onto the Spiral Ramp → down to the leafcutter highway. Skips the outward-facing castle. The party may not know it exists.
2. **Flying** — off any landing pad (Ballroom 1, Ballroom 2, Great Hall). Two flavors:
   - **Pre-arranged butterfly** — as performers, convince a butterfly on standby (*"someone else hired a butterfly, he's sitting on the platform… we just finished our gig, take us back to the city for an extra"*). He'll haggle but do it.
   - **Tackle a butterfly off the ledge** — *"book it off the ramp and tackle a butterfly off the edge"* on a good roll; the butterfly flaps to not die and can't stop the fall. (DM riff: *"What the fuck, dude — you're pulling us both down!"*) Bugs don't take real fall damage in-fiction, *"but this is D&D and we make the rules."*
   - **Fall-damage ruling — DECIDED.** An unassisted jump is not survivable-by-default. Normal 5e fall damage applies, *"but it's not just the fall damage — it's falling into the Overgrowth"* below: *"if you jump from the castle into the tree canopy it's still not gonna end well… it might be fatal."* The palace reads as *"essentially a castle on a cliff… it is up."* **Requirement: to go off an edge on purpose they must have some way to slow or guide the fall** — *"they've gotta have some way to slow or guide their fall. For sure."* (i.e. the butterfly, the makeshift parachute, or a spell.) DM read on likelihood: *"I don't think the players are just gonna fuckin' send it off the edge. That doesn't sound like something they would do."*
3. **The main entrance** — the Spiral Ramp, i.e. the servants'/ground-bug entrance up from the leafcutter highway (fortified servants' gate w/ watchtower per S07). From the core you can go straight down the ramp.

Additional improvised outs raised: set a fire / create a big distraction / blow a hole, get lost in the scramble; a **makeshift parachute** or **hostage butterfly** off a balcony.

# Background — Arc Material (not session beats)

## A. The Brood Coven & the Forbidden Magic — DECIDED

The DMs reconstructed and then settled the campaign's central backstory this pass. They flagged it as the thing they'd been circling for a while: *"that's what was dark. That's what we earned, is that explanation. That nugget."* This is what the Leafcutter Queen can reveal in §3b.

### The Brood Coven

- **The name is chosen.** *"The paradigm of a witch coven isn't too much to use. It doesn't have to be a blood coven. We could call it a **brood coven**."* — *"I love brood coven. I love that name."*
- **What it is:** the ant queens *"are more powerful than other insects because they are whole hives unto themselves."* The coven bonds them: *"they are, by some magical property, bonded."* Practically, *"they each got a little bit — could borrow a little bit of magic power from one another."*
- **The upside was real, and mutual:** *"they had kind of all agreed that this sharing meant that they could all prosper together."* *"They lived in a balance."*
- **The downside only surfaced later:** *"they were all linked… their fate is now tied together."*
- **Crucially, the pact is not the magic.** *"The pact isn't the magic. The Black Queen did some bad magic, and it rippled down the pact."* The pact's own term was a prohibition: *"a pact to not delve too deep into the magic."*

### The forbidden magic is TIME MAGIC

The load-bearing decision of this transcript:

> *"I think we could just say that all time magic is forbidden and evil, because nature is all about the passage of time."*
> — *"Yes, dude. That's it. I love that."*

### What the Black Queen actually did

The DMs recovered and then ratified the original version:

- She *"was attacked by a wolf spider infected by cordyceps, got bitten, and froze time so she didn't get infected, and her whole colony didn't fall."*
- **It overshot.** *"Rather than just what she wanted to do — which was pause the progress of the infection in her — instead it paused the world in these expanding waves."*
- **Two consequences:** the calamity itself, and *"it also succeeded in pausing the infection, which lets her control Cordyceps"* — i.e. *"she is super powerful and can control cordyceps-infected stuff."* This is the in-world reason cordyceps enemies exist at all: *"my problem with it originally was it gave no motivation for them to have any cordyceps things to fight, but that does give us that. So yeah, I'm good with that."*
- **Her motive was survival, not conquest.** *"The goal, in my head, the goal was not so much a power grab as an attempt to survive."* The DMs deliberately steered away from a straightforwardly evil version: *"I kind of like having the big bad not be just evil."*
- **The madness came after.** *"When they break the pact, they go a little crazy"* — *"the act of trying to save herself and a colony also kind of drives her crazy."*

### The ripple through the coven

*"Because they were all bonded by the brood coven, they all suffered consequences. And they all went a little crazy and got more powerful."* This is the single mechanism behind every warped queen — the Red Ant Queen's fire-and-vanity mania included (§3b).

### Endgame shape (as currently understood)

- The Black Queen is defeatable — *"an evil person who you can defeat and just turn everything off"* — **or** persuadable: *"maybe they can somehow convince her to undo it."*
- **The real big bad is the cordyceps wolf spider.** *"There still should be a big bad, so… maybe there's this big cordyceps spider."*

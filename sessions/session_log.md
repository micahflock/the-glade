# The Glade — Session Log

## Session 0 — The Beginning


The party assembled at the Chitinholm branch of the Royal Jelly Society, summoned by a general call to bugs of action. In a cramped upstairs room repurposed as a war room, they were briefed by the Sub-Committee on Impeding Winter Doom: Francesca "Boodle" Booloticia IV (merchant guild, jewel beetle), Dr. T'wigfingers (scientist, stickbug), and Sven (#7834, ant drone). The committee presented their theory: the unnatural cold gripping the Glade is not weather — it is a shockwave expanding from a single epicenter. Boodle's tip came from missing trade shipments; T'wigfingers had cross-referenced refugee data to establish directionality; Sven reported collapsed agricultural yields and raiding parties. The party was tasked with traveling to a lost RJS branch to retrieve records that, combined with Chitinholm's data, would triangulate a heading toward the source. In exchange: RJS membership, a title, and the legend. The session ended with the party moving through Chitinholm's market square.

## Session 1 — On the Road

Boodle made her exit immediately, leaving T'wigfingers to brief the party and hand off the sealed triangulation tube. After a pass through the RJS stockroom and stables — where Drix won over Bessie the sowbug with a walnut-shell lute performance — the party loaded up and headed east through Chitinholm. They passed through the packed market square without stopping, Nancy caught talk of dead farms near the gate, and Drix earned a coin or two busking on the road out. On the frozen road east, they ran into a family of shield bugs fleeing toward Chitinholm and then a cart under attack by earwig bandits. Drix called the bandits out first and took two rocks for it. The fight ended with Tabby's crits, Nancy's Sleep spell, Drix's Dissonant Whispers and Hold Person, and Nancy landing the killing blow on the leader — the rest scattered. The cart driver Bert ran past the party without a word, straight back to Chitinholm. The pillbug still hitched to the wagon didn't bolt — just watched. The party kept her.

## Session 2 — The Broken Wagon

The party repaired the damaged cart with a scavenged leaf for an axle, packed the bearing with grease, and pulled the broken wheel entirely — leaving a functional tri-wheeled rig. They swapped pack animals: Bessie was hitched to the repaired cart with a note from Drix tucked in the cargo, sent back toward Chitinholm on her own. Bestie, the pillbug from the wagon, came with the party. Camp was made between the root flares off the road; Tabby scouted, Nancy scoped the trail from high ground, Drix played lute and settled the site. The party finally opened T'wigfingers' sealed tube — data, not drama; a top-down map of Chitinholm annotated with lines and notations — and Tabby and Nancy began a physical reference map. Tabby took third watch and caught a faint glow to the east just before sunrise, which she marked on the map; by morning it was clear the glow was the village burning ahead of them. The next day brought the burnt village: no bodies, no food, only soot and Lars, the earwig who had run in Session 1, now fractured and barely present. He named two ant types — black and red — and pointed them northeast, where the bodies were dragged. The party left him with what he already had and moved on. Antennington was ransacked but standing; the RJS building yielded a note in green ink pinned with a hand-pickaxe, and Nancy found the Tune Hammer of Amber in the lab. At the Antennington Amber Mine, Ricko the ox beetle ran the lift, and Garett the green weevil waited on Level 2 with his triangulation parchment and a glowing magic amber rock — Ahganmin — which he pressed into Nancy's hands. Garett floated the possibility that the Bloom isn't a fluctuation of known magic at all but something new, and that Antennington sits at the edge of its effect. The party left the mine with three data sources now combined on their map and a heading toward the Bloom's source.

## Session 3 — The Body Cart

The party departed the Antennington Amber Mine after Nancy spent the night studying the Bloom with Garett, who built a detection box for the Ahganmin before they left. On the road east, fresh cart tracks in the mud confirmed ant traffic. The party went off-road and used Drix's Locate Animals or Plants to confirm ants ahead. Around a bend: a broken cart loaded with charred bodies, two fire ant commanders bullying four leafcutter workers into fixing a wheel. The party engaged — leafcutters fought against the party halfheartedly on the fire ants' orders, and as combat turned, some switched sides. All fire ants were killed, no leafcutters lost. In the aftermath, a loyalist leafcutter panicked about being implicated while a rebel declared the fire ants better dead, revealing the existence of a fungal farm growing food on corpses and a nascent resistance movement inside. The leafcutters also named "The Barrier" — a line across the land where nothing rots on one side and nothing grows on the other — and pointed out its location for the party to mark on their map, and framed the fire ants' grip as "protection" offered in exchange for following their direction. Nancy approached the loyalist directly — "We are on a mission to understand this bloom, might makes right doesn't sit with me" — leaving him cowed but unpersuaded, and promised the leafcutters the party would help protect them in exchange for cooperation. The session ended with the party planning to infiltrate the Winter Farm: play dead in the body cart, Drix disguised as a fire ant.

## Session 4 — The Winter Farm

The party traveled to the Winter Farm with their leafcutter guides as the road froze and snow began to fall. Nancy flagged the red ant bodies were still unnaturally warm long after death. Before the ridge, Nancy used Message to threaten the loyalist Greg — "I'm watching you and I will kill you if you act out" — and the party tied Bestie up with a snack and a blanket above the valley. From behind a pine cone on the ridge, they looked down into a misted valley with a frozen stream and three or four wagon-width entrances dug into the far bank. Drix cast Disguise Self to become a red ant; Nancy and Tabby used the disguise kit to play dead in the cart. On the descent Greg broke and bolted across the river, but Drix — in character as a red ant — pre-emptively yelled that Greg was "acting crazy, fungus in his brain." Greg vanished into the workers with his credibility destroyed. Drix bluffed past a single red ant at the gate and the party rolled inside. Deep in the leafcutter-only tunnels, Allen confirmed that the Sporemother did not like the fire ants; Drix dropped the disguise and Allen led them through fungus chambers and past the fresh-leaf room — "we cut those weeks ago, they should be mulch by now" — to a small processing chamber with a living fungal curtain. The puzzle ran long and beautiful: Mage Hand tickled, Drix's lullaby made the curtain relax and Drix cry, Nancy cut a piece (it keened), Drix cast Healing Word to quiet it, and finally Tabby asked the sympathetic leafcutter how the last person had passed through — soft, then stomp, then hush — and the curtain parted. Beyond it, Sporemother Vell and her rebel cell welcomed the party at a small round table. Vell confirmed the source of the Bloom, alluded to other species receiving amplified powers, and gave them her mandate: bring the news to the leafcutter queen so she can choose to break from the alliance on her own terms. The older cell assistant carved Runes of Fungal Protection into each PC — Nancy on the top-right pauldron, Drix as a "tramp stamp" on the lower back, Tabby on the back of the left shoulder. A leafcutter rebel named Antshley volunteered to escort them to the queen. The party left through a tunnel that descended under the barrier, warmth replacing cold without any visible threshold, and gained three levels (Level 3 → Level 6) as they stepped into the Overgrowth. Greg sucks. Allen rules.

## Session 5 — The City in the Overgrowth

The party crossed from the Winter Farm tunnel into the Overgrowth and felt the level-up in their bodies — Nancy clutched at something under his clothes, Tabby moved more at ease and feline, Drix played a counter-charm on the walnut shell lute that landed on Nancy hard enough to trigger a happy freakout, and the Ahganmin glowed bright enough in the new ambient magic that Nancy fashioned a turban with the amber set in as the gem. Antshley walked them through the gradient tunnel — dirt walls becoming wet living vegetation, illuminating fungus giving way to god-rays through a latticed ceiling — and stopped before the daylight mouth. She handed Tabby the navi-gus (a smooth translucent ball of fungus keyed to Canopia, responsive to leafcutter pheromone trails), recited the dead-drop method at the Dewdrop Inn, and turned back. The road north went past nectar-fat caterpillars, weevils riding a millipede in nine saddles, and a fire-ant escort whose body shimmered with rising heat. They reached the Weevilville and met Twyla, the diminutive weevil aid coordinator, who let Nancy and Tabby help hand out fungus rations to a long line of detritivores; Twyla shared what she knew (or assumed) about the alliance, gave them directions to the Dewdrop Inn, and mentioned the Mayflyer. Up the trunk-spiraling ramp and through the gate of Canopia, Nancy bought roses for the whole party and they ducked a yelling drunk bee. They detoured into the Queen's Crown casino, announced a heist, sat at a high-rollers' wasp table, and tried to spread a rumor that the fire ants are growing fungus on dead bodies — the wasps did not care about groundling problems, the rumor flopped, and security walked them out (Nancy lifted 2 sap and 1 cig on the way). They passed the Royal Jelly Society's Canopia branch without entering. At the Dewdrop Inn the dead drop ran clean: ginger honeycap, sip-wince, "it's a hot batch," back booth. A clumsy hooded figure stumbled in, bumped their table, dropped a key, and went up to the second floor. The party followed and met Gleam — an earnest, beady-eyed weevil with no talent for subterfuge whom the table immediately declared "THE CUTEST." Gleam confirmed Vell's broader network, told them the Leafcutter Queen knows something is wrong but is constrained by a bad deal, and offered to get them into the hive through his contacts. Session ended mid-conversation, before Gleam gave them a concrete plan.

## Session 6 — Orchid Assassin

Gleam delivered the full handoff in the upstairs room: the Red Ant Queen's fete in a few days, an invitation via the weevil channel, a band slot for the party (Drix's reputation), the Leafcutter Queen paraded and then escorted back to her chambers as the window to slip away, and six specialist contacts already placed in the palace (Tudd the leafcutter major, Demicous the meek minim, Vikko the jewel-beetle bartender, Diedre the lacewing performer, Dax the cockroach MMA medic, Zalle the sawfly-larva pheromone mixologist) — three reachable by dead drop before the event. He caught his robe in the door on the way out. The party took the room, long-rested, rigged a sock on the doorknob as an improvised alarm. In the morning, Dale the bartender served them breakfast and Beeatrix — the grieving, hungover mining bee who'd been singing in the background of Session 5 — woke up squawking about the monster haunting her ancestral flower meadow. Nancy made the table-altering pivot of recruiting Beeatrix, who used to "sing the classics," to front the band at the fete. The party went to western Canopia (where the Red Ant Queen's palace is visible across the canopy) to find an east-bound flight and met Barbra, an Eastern Comma butterfly nursing a hot water after losing all her money at the casino. She agreed to fly them close to the meadow for 2 sap — would not land, knew someone "swallowed by a flower in an instant," and mentioned offhand that she'd felt stronger since the Bloom. Nancy wove flight harnesses for himself and Tabby; Drix flew alongside. Barbra dropped them on an Iris leaf at the meadow's edge and agreed to come back at evening. At the meadow they saw no signs of struggle but spotted an egg sack clinging to the underside of a leaf; sneaking up the leaves and stems, they recognized it as orchid mantis. Something shifted beside them and grabbed for Drix — Drix strummed his guitar, the mantis missed, battle ensued. Tabby and Nancy together killed one nymph before the session ended, roughly halfway through the encounter. Mid-combat cliffhanger.

## Session 6.5 — The Mantis Felled

A short continuation session, picking up mid-combat where Session 6 had paused. The party fought through the mama orchid mantis's flower camouflage, her adjacent-only hops, and the grapple-and-crush move, finishing the remaining nymphs and bringing the mama down. They won — the meadow east of Canopia was cleared. Each adventurer claimed a trophy from the kill: Drix and Nancy each took a mandible, which became The Mantible — an invisible finesse blade; Tabby harvested mantis spines to craft into daggers. As evening fell, Barbra the Eastern Comma butterfly returned to the meadow's edge as promised and flew the party back to Canopia. The reward Beeatrix owes them for clearing her ancestral meadow went uncollected — left for the next time they cross paths in the city — and the fete arc waits ahead: dead-drop specialist picks, Beeatrix fronting the band, and Gleam's first-dark pickup.

## Session 7 — Heist Prep in Canopia

With the mantis dead and the fete looming tomorrow night, the party spent a full day in Canopia gathering intel and setting up advantages for the coming heist. Looking for a smithy and finding none in so upscale a district, Nancy got a cockroach with fine gem-work to point them at the Ruby Thorax (west side, likely closed) and to mention "two entrances," plus a lead on Trader Twizzlers for fete outfits. At the Dewdrop they found Beeatrix on stage mid-lament — a guilt-soaked song about sending them to their deaths — and Drix joined on his lute while Nancy answered with an interpretive dance, turning grief into reunion. Overjoyed they'd survived, Beeatrix locked in as the band's singer (she plays a lyre; Nancy on tap shoes and bells) and pressed on Drix the last of her late mother's Stick-All Honey, an heirloom that bonds one thing to one thing forever, in a single use; the gold she owed was waived. The band went west to the promenade, where the Red Ant Queen's palace rose into breathtaking view, and busked among the treat stands. A chatty honeybun vendor gave up the day's best intel — her brother Johnathy works the palace kitchens for the fete, and the servants' quarters run under the ballrooms — while Tabby worked the crowd for overheard layout (two ballrooms, a fighting pit, a banquet hall, and a balcony-less throne room) and seeded a rumor, reinforced by Drix, that the fete would be open to everyone at 8:30pm. They talked Beeatrix into going home sober rather than on to the casino, then scoped the palace's grand level: goods streaming in, guards every thirty feet, a fortified servants' gate with a watchtower, enclosed kitchen-to-quarters walkways, and — the standout — carts of juniper berries, scorpion stingers, and glowing goop, spell components that don't obviously combine, as if someone were trying to work something out. Drix cast Locate Animals or Plants on the berries. The session ended with the party banking it all, headed back to the Dewdrop to draw up blueprints — the fete now one night away, their dead-drop specialist picks still unmade, and the prepped Ruby Thorax, Vikki's Volatiles, and Queen Bee Casino scenes left on the shelf.

## Session 7.5 — The Last Day in Canopia

The morning of the fete, and the party finally went to the shops. They woke at the Dewdrop to find Dale had laid out all their favorite foods and that Beeatrix had come back early and sober the night before — she slept through the entire session in the inn's kitchen, and Nancy took her measurements without waking her. Drix opened the day by sending an Invisible Servant to hold their place in the queue at Trader Twizzlers, which was walked back at the table as a rules correction, so they simply walked to the shop and lied their way past forty waiting bugs by claiming to work there. It worked, and it made somebody else soil themselves. Inside, in a cocoon chair on the floor of a three-storey seed pod, Twizzlers the bagworm moth asked *them* for a performance rather than the other way around, liked what she heard, and dressed all four of them: asymmetric purple for Nancy, black-to-purple ombre bellbottoms and a capelet for Drix, a gown for the sleeping Beeatrix, and for Tabby — who asked for the least flashy thing in the building — something all black and spiky with secret pockets sewn in. At the Ruby Thorax, Nancy pounded on the door until an elderly green stick bug answered, and Thoralex turned out to be the day's real prize. He showed them the coronation crown he is finishing, the centerpiece of that night's ceremony, mentioned almost in passing that he had been invited into the Red Queen's own chambers to fit it, and then walked them through her quarters from memory: servants' entrance, up the ramp, the great hall, stairs to the throne room, the living quarters behind the throne, a spiral up to her apartment, a long walk from the kitchen — and, once past the throne, the growing sense that it was less a palace than a fortress. One room, he said, smelled odd and rumbled and sizzled. He took their commission too, and the party split three ways on it: Nancy and Drix kept the invisibility on their Mantibles, while Tabby traded it away on both of her mantis-spine daggers for one master's edge and one balanced edge, choosing damage over stealth on the eve of a stealth job. Nancy took a sketch of the crown on the way out. Walking to the Royal Jelly Society they noticed a scorched, boarded-up building sitting wrong on a pretty street — Vikki's Volatiles, open for business — and went in out of curiosity, which turned into the session's other windfall. Vikki, a grumbling bombardier beetle bossing around Ricky her house centipede, confirmed she supplies the wagons rolling to the castle, complained that her stock has been exploding and behaving strangely, and named the destination outright: the Leafcutter Queen's lab. They left armed with two exploding peppercorns, smoke-bomb makings, and a pinch of purple salt to turn fire purple. The Royal Jelly Society was last, and it did exactly what it was built to do. Bees met them at the door with water to wash their hands and a tray of candy, the walls were hung with hundreds of framed and dated flowers, and when the party said they had come from Chitinholm the branch's complaint was that Chitinholm's letters had arrived with no petals in them. The hostess sat by the fire and asked eagerly what they had seen while traveling, meaning flowers, and had no interest whatsoever in the blight elsewhere; Heather's notes render the verdict in underlined capitals. She passed them through to the archive, where Mr. Pollendew worked from a ladder amid journals and portfolios and a desk piled with opened mail and half-written replies he cannot bring himself to send, and pointed them toward a missive from Chitinholm. In an old cabinet they found a dusty jar: *Aged Royal Jelly, Bitmore batch, one dose, do not waste.* The session ended as they stepped back out into the daylight, dressed and armed and re-bladed, with the fete that night, the Queen's Crown and the fighting gym still unvisited, and — because they never made it back to the inn — Gleam's runner still to come, the specialist roster still unhanded, and no flyer booked to get them out.

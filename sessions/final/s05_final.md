# Session 5 — The City in the Overgrowth
*Final session notes — reconciled with DM and player play notes*

---

## Cold Open

The party crossed the threshold from the Winter Farm tunnel into the Overgrowth and felt the level-up in their bodies before they knew what they were feeling. Their carapaces went warm and started thrumming. Nancy clutched at something under his clothes. Tabby found herself moving with new feline ease. Drix lifted the walnut shell lute and played a counter-charm of a song that hit Nancy like a sunbeam — Nancy lost his mind a little, in the best way. The Ahganmin in Nancy's possession started glowing brighter than it ever had; Nancy ended up wearing it set into a turban as the gem.

Antshley waited for them to get their feet under them, then kept walking.

---

## The Tunnel Becomes the Mat

The tunnel walls shifted from packed dirt to layers of wet, living vegetation. The illuminating fungus gave way to a subtle emerald glow. The floor went from earth to something firm, damp, and slightly bouncy. The air went sharp and fresh — no more dark sweetness of decay. God rays began piercing the lattice of the ceiling. They were no longer underground; they were tunneling through the green mat that *was* the new ground level of the Overgrowth.

Antshley stopped well before the daylit mouth of the tunnel and motioned them close.

> "This is where I leave you. It's too risky for me to be topside — we can't risk exposing our cell. Your next contact is in Canopia."

She handed **Tabby** a twisted ball of smooth translucent fungus — the **navi-gus** — and explained: it responds to the pheromone trails the leafcutter roadworkers leave. This one is keyed to Canopia. It can be re-keyed. The paths are constantly being re-cut.

> "I don't know who you're looking for. I just know how to reach them. Get to the Dewdrop Inn. Order a ginger honeycap, over hard. Take a sip, wince, and say *'It's a hot batch.'* Hand it back to the bartender. They'll apologize and tell you where to wait. Your rune will prove your association with us."

She said her goodbyes — *"You've given us hope — hope that we don't struggle just to be met with starvation"* — and watched them walk into the daylight without her.

---

## The Road to Canopia

They emerged onto a narrow path neatly paved with circular slices of foliage, stems and leaves rising into a dense forest of growth that limited vision in every direction. Above them, a slim slice of sky bracketed by green. To the side of the path, no solid surface — just tangled vegetation falling away into darkness.

The navi-gus twitched and throbbed in response to the correct pheromone trail. They followed it.

The path crossed a much wider thoroughfare. While they hesitated and watched, the bugs of the Overgrowth went past them: a merry crowd of caterpillars, fat with growth, chatting excitedly; a serious knot of weevils riding nine saddles in a row down the back of a millipede. The color and gaiety after the bleak chill of the Winter Farm landed as a shock.

Atmosphere on the road:

- **Other travelers** — bugs on foot in both directions, none alarmed.
- **A leafcutter road crew** cutting back a tangled section, with **two fire ants** in fluorescent orange and pink petal robes nearby, shimmering with a visible heat haze. The fire ants were fidgety, glancing skyward.
- **Aerial traffic** — through gaps in the canopy: butterflies and bees moving in unprecedented numbers. A dragonfly flickered through a cloud of bees in iridescent lightning, leaving glittering shards of carapace behind it.

---

## The Weevilville

The leafcutter road widened into a broad plaza, kept clear by the ants. The trunk of an enormous tree defined the far side, and a leafway ramp spiraled up around it toward the canopy and the lights of a city. A humble, hard-up shanty town filled the clearing — homes cut into the vegetation itself, raw edges where the leafcutters had trimmed them. A sign at the entry: **Welcome to Weevilville!**

A familiar wooden cart was being unloaded in the square. Leafcutters in neat ranks stacked bundles of pale fungus. A red ant sat at the cart's head in a delicate petal shroud, fidgety and twitchy, attention darting between leafcutters, sky, and the line of detritivores. Moving through it all with a bark board of tally marks was a tiny weevil in a clean apron, calling families up by name.

### Twyla

The weevil's name was **Twyla**. She let Nancy and Tabby help pass out bundles of fungus to the line — *"If you're detritivores and hungry, you've come to the right place"* — and was happy to chat as they worked.

What Twyla shared:

- The fungus comes from "a farm on the outside" — she assumes it's made from leaves.
- **Fire ants protect the leafcutters from predation**, which has gotten worse along with the rest of the Overgrowth.
- **Leafcutters are the backbone of the new normal**, feeding detritivores and keeping the roads open.
- **Nectar-eaters and fresh-veggie-eaters are doing great** (and the things that eat them are doing great too). Many of the displaced detritivores have found work in Canopia keeping the city running.
- She personally **likes to gamble at the Queen's Crown**.
- She gave the party **directions to the Dewdrop Inn** and mentioned **the Mayflyer** in passing.

---

## Up the Ramp

The ramp wound the trunk of the tree and rose past the highest growth. Below them, the green mat stretched in broad swaths of colorful flowers nearly glowing in the moonlight, and the Weevilville's lights were just barely visible. Above them, the city grew brighter — a complex geometry of crisp wax comb and flowing paper-wasp spires, protruding both upwards and downwards from the crook between two massive branches. Fat cocoons and chrysalises hung from the underside of it all, bathed in the light of hundreds of multicolored lanterns. They reached the gate.

---

## Canopia

Canopia at night: light and sound, even late. A constant thrum of conversation and wings. Elaborate apartments. Beautifully planted gardens. Flowers tucked into every corner. Streets swept clean. Bees thrumming overhead — a few of them too inebriated to fly, stumbling nectar-drunk down the green leaf streets. The city spiraled upwards, alight and abuzz.

**Nancy bought roses for the whole party.**

A drunk bee yelled at them from somewhere in the throng — they ducked it cleanly and kept moving.

### The Queen's Crown

They detoured into the city's casino — *Very lively.* The party announced (to themselves, mostly) that they wanted to do a heist, sat down at a table, and pivoted from a pickpocket attempt into trying to spread a rumor: that the fire ants are growing fungus on dead bodies. Their audience was a table of high-rolling wasps. The wasps did not care about groundling problems. The rumor flopped flat.

The party flubbed the move enough that **security escorted them out**. On the way out, Nancy lifted **2 sap and 1 cig** from a pocket. The party engaged with the heist concept, said they liked it, and left the door explicitly open to coming back for another attempt.

### Past the Royal Jelly Society

On the route between the Queen's Crown and the Dewdrop Inn, they passed a branch of the **Royal Jelly Society** — and walked past it. (No entry. The dissonance scene goes back on the shelf.)

## The Dewdrop Inn

Rowdy. Rough. Fun. A large stained-leaf window above the entrance shed multicolored shadows on the floor. To the right, a long wooden slab bar; to the left, six or seven tables made from the leaves of different kinds of trees, each one full.

The party went straight to the bar and ran the move:

1. Ordered a **ginger honeycap, over hard**.
2. Sipped, winced, said **"It's a hot batch."**
3. Handed the drink back.

The bartender — mustachioed, professional, unflappable — apologized profusely, slipped in something about a special guest, and directed them to a back booth where he'd bring a replacement.

### The Hooded Figure

While they waited, a clumsy figure in a long robe entered the tavern, stumbled to their table, **bumped into it**, and kept walking — straight up to the **second floor**. They left a small wooden room key on the party's table.

The party finished (or didn't finish) their drinks and followed the key upstairs.

---

## Gleam

They opened the door of an upstairs room and found a figure in a long brown robe seated facing a small fire, hood up. The figure stood, turned, pulled the hood back.

It was an extremely earnest-looking weevil. Beady eyes. Long, expressive snout. Absolutely no talent for subterfuge. He beamed at them.

> "I'm glad you could make it."

His name is **Gleam**. The table's verdict was immediate and unanimous: *Gleam = THE CUTEST.*

The party told him they wanted to help. Gleam confirmed:

- He is allied with the **rebel ants** and Vell's broader network.
- The **Leafcutter Queen knows something is wrong** — but she is constrained, made a bad deal, and is looking for a way out.
- He can get them into **the hive** through his contacts.

Session ended partway through that conversation.

## Session-Established Worldbuilding (canon now)

- **Canopia** is a pollinator city built in the crook of a single huge tree — bees and wasps, wax comb and paper-wasp spires, multicolored lanterns at night. Streets swept; gardens perfect; pollinators drunk and territorial year-round; the alliance is popular here as civic infrastructure.
- **The Weevilville** is the shanty town at the base of Canopia's tree, where displaced detritivores live. Named for the weevils running humanitarian aid. Fungus is rationed in by the leafcutter/fire-ant alliance — leafcutters unload, a fire-ant supervisor watches, a weevil runs the line.
- **The Overgrowth mat** is the new ground level. Tunnels and roads are cut through living vegetation. Bugs navigate by **navi-gus** — a piece of reactive fungus that responds to leafcutter pheromone trails.
- **Fire ants run hot enough to throw visible heat shimmer.** Confirmed in daylight on the road.
- **Twyla** runs aid distribution in the Weevilville. **Gleam** is the Canopia-side rebel cell contact.
- **The Queen's Crown** is the city's casino.
- **The Mayflyer** is an upscale bar on the main road; **A Corner** is a smaller jazz spot — both passed but not entered.
- **The Royal Jelly Society** has a Canopia branch — passed but not entered.
- **The Dewdrop Inn** is a rowdy tavern with a stained-leaf window, a long wooden bar, and an unflappable mustachioed bartender running a rebel dead drop.
